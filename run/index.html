03 Story

Six days on a bus with a renowned designer

Kia Designers' Europe Workshop
Local Manager · 2024
Germany · France · Switzerland
11 junior designers

The pitch. Many agencies were bidding. As the local manager I presented about half of our pitch — a team on the ground in Germany, and a tour that would move, not just ride a bus from museum to museum.

The idea. Car design was turning back to heritage, and so was the group's next design direction. So I asked: what would a junior designer want to learn from a veteran — by experience, not in a lecture? The answer was a renowned retired car designer, whom I reached through an acquaintance's boss and convinced by email. He rode the bus with the group for all six days.

Making it active. The participants were in their mid-20s to mid-30s, so three days were behind the wheel: 5 rental cars, teams of 3, winding roads. I built four routes in Google Maps, drove them myself, and kept one. Every car had a radio; the rental company added special insurance. Other stops: the Nürburgring, the Vitra Design Museum and car museums along the route.

When it rained. On the driving day the rain slowed everything down. The Nürburgring passenger ride was off — we swapped it for well-known spots on the way.

How it landed. Kia's participant survey came back positive, Kia chose the same agency again, and I was asked back for 2026 (I said no — exams).

What I'm still thinking about. You can't please everyone on a tour like this. Aim for the majority, or for the average? I went with the average — nervous drivers rode with the ones who couldn't get enough of driving. I'm still not sure there's a right answer. And next time I'll plan the pace for the speaker too, not just the participants.

04 Story

Five affiliates, three carmakers, one timetable

Automotive roadshow for Samsung
Local Manager · 02–09/2026
3 German carmaker sites

The job. Local operations for a roadshow where five Samsung affiliates took part, at three German carmakers' sites — about 300 visitors and 30 VIPs per event, with an on-site team of 5 promoters and 3 staff.

Finding the people. Job ads in Korean, German and English, written separately for each city's applicants; profile sheets; final picks made together with the client. Lesson learned early: agree the hiring criteria with the person who works with the team every day, not only with the one who signs off.

Inside the hall. The site had two halves: outdoor setup, and the indoor stage build and rehearsal. The client was short of manager-level people, so they gave me the indoor half. I re-divided who stands where and does what, and rewrote the timetable. I heard the executives' VIP pitches for the first time at the rehearsal, and each had a different style: I placed the livelier speakers where attention tends to drop, and kept slow, even-toned speakers apart rather than back to back. I also re-ordered the VIP tour so each affiliate got the attention its stake in the event deserved.

What I'd tell my past self. Settle overtime rules when you sign — on-site days grow.
